The venue is the single largest line item in a wedding budget and the first thing couples book. It determines the date, the guest count, the logistics, and half of every other vendor decision that follows. Mistakes here are the most expensive to fix — swapping a photographer a month out is possible, swapping a venue almost never is.

## Start with the guest list, not the photos

The usual sequence is seeing a beautiful hall on Instagram and going to visit. The right sequence is drafting a guest list first, even a rough one.

Armenian weddings count differently than a typical Western wedding. An invitation goes to a family, not a person. Cousins, neighbors, parents' coworkers, godparents — the list grows on its own, and 250–400 guests is the norm here, not the exception. If you're touring a venue for 150 without first sizing up the real number, you're probably wasting a visit.

Draft three lists: essential, likely, possible. The floor is list one, the ceiling is all three combined. Search for a venue against the ceiling.

## Stated capacity vs. real capacity

Venues quote a maximum — how many people physically fit at tables. That number usually doesn't include:

- the dance floor, which needs to be large and in use most of the evening at an Armenian wedding;
- a stage for musicians and space for equipment;
- a cake area and a gift table;
- the aisle space waitstaff need between tables.

Rule of thumb: subtract 15–20% from the stated capacity. A venue listed "for 400" comfortably seats 320–340 with a proper dance floor.

Ask specifically how tables will be arranged for your guest count and request a floor plan. Round tables seating 10–12 take up more space than long banquet tables but make conversation easier.

## Location and logistics

In Yerevan, choosing a district isn't only about prestige.

**Kentron.** Convenient for guests — many arrive on their own or by taxi. The downside is parking. If the venue doesn't have its own lot for 60–80 cars, the evening starts with half your guests circling the block.

**Outskirts and the suburbs.** More space, often a garden and a view, cheaper per guest. The downside is you need transfers, and that has to be budgeted. If it's a half-hour drive from the church to the venue, the whole day's timing shifts.

Drive the route yourself, on the same day of the week and at the same time as the wedding. Friday traffic in Yerevan behaves differently than Sunday traffic.

## What's actually included in the price

The main source of unpleasant surprises. Ask item by item:

- **Is the rental fee included in the per-person menu price, or charged separately?** Venues often quote a per-guest rate while still charging a separate hall fee.
- **Service.** How many waiters per table, and is their service included in the bill.
- **Alcohol.** Can you bring your own, and is there a corkage fee. This is common practice in Armenia, and the price difference is significant.
- **Décor.** Which tables, linens, and chairs are included by default, and what you'll need to order from a florist.
- **Sound and lighting.** Does the venue have its own equipment and technician, or do musicians bring everything themselves.
- **Cake.** Is there a cutting-and-serving fee if the cake is ordered elsewhere.
- **Overtime.** Until what hour the rental runs and what each extra hour costs. Armenian weddings rarely end early.

## Questions people forget to ask

**How many weddings happen there in one day?** If the venue has two halls and both are booked, you're sharing parking, the entrance, and staff attention.

**Who manages your evening, and will they actually be there?** The person who sold you the venue and the person running the floor on the day are often not the same.

**What's the plan if it rains?** If part of the evening is planned on a terrace or in a garden, there needs to be an actual backup, not "it's usually dry this time of year."

**Is there a separate room for the bride?** A place to change, fix makeup, and sit for ten minutes matters more by mid-evening than it seems during the venue tour.

**How's the air conditioning?** July and August in Yerevan hit +35°C, and three hundred people in a hall add their own heat on top of that.

## The contract

Get in writing: date, time, guest count with an acceptable margin, total price, deposit amount and schedule, cancellation and refund terms, and the menu.

Separately, ask what happens if fewer guests show up than booked. Most venues charge for the minimum agreed headcount regardless of actual attendance. That's standard — but better to know in advance.

## How far ahead to book

Good venues in Yerevan for June through September get booked out roughly a year in advance. If the wedding falls in peak season, start looking 10–12 months out. For off-season dates, six months is usually enough.

Touring more than five or six venues in person stops being useful — after that they blur together in memory. Narrow the list by capacity, location, and budget first, and save in-person visits for the finalists.
