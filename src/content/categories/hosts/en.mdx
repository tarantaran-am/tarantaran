## A tamada or a host for an Armenian wedding

At an Armenian wedding the tamada is effectively the evening's second director. The feast is built around toasts: to the parents, to the couple, to the families, to those who are not there. The tamada keeps the order of toasts, knows who should speak, and can work a room of 250–400 guests. A European-style host focuses on the programme and interaction. Many today combine both.

## Languages

In Yerevan a tamada usually leads the evening in at least two languages, Armenian and Russian, and sometimes English when relatives from the diaspora attend. Check how easily they switch between languages.

## How to choose

- ask for the full video of at least one toast, not only a showreel;
- discuss the tone: formal and traditional, or light and humorous;
- ask how the tamada prepares the script and works with the DJ and musicians;
- check whether there is a replacement in case of illness.

Good tamadas are booked eight to ten months ahead for the peak season, June to September. Above are wedding tamadas and hosts in Yerevan and across Armenia.
